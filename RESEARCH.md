# Color Vision Field Guide: editorial evidence and coverage

Last reviewed October 13, 2026. The guide is educational and its screen interactions have no clinical validity. Education, not medical advice.

## What each section covers

| Section | What it explains | Limits stated on the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The moment | Why one person can see a figure on a color plate while another sees only dots, and why effort is the wrong question | One plate is not a diagnosis, a type or a score |
| The tests | Color plates, mirror letter chart, stereo test, bright-light fundus check, hue-cap ordering, anomaloscope | Each test answers a different question; web demos are not tests |

## Editorial boundaries

Do not publish a child's name, exam findings, plate responses, scores, subtype, genotype or records. Do not present a family story as a genetic result, a camouflage finding as a general advantage, or a job rule as universal. Shorthand prevalence figures must carry their population.

## Adjacent methods and technology

A separate site section introduces HRR plates, D-15 versus 100 Hue, computer-based threshold methods, lantern tests, and genetic testing. Filter glasses, color-identification tools, and investigational gene therapy are discussed with NEI's limits. This is an orientation to method families, not an exhaustive catalogue of every commercial edition or a clinical test order.
